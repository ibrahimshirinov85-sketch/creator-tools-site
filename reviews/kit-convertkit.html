Kit (Formerly ConvertKit) Review: Worth Switching To in 2026?

Kit's rebrand from ConvertKit came with a sharper focus: it's built specifically for people who make a living from an audience — newsletter writers, course creators, and independent publishers — rather than marketers running campaigns for a business.

This site does not currently have an active affiliate relationship with Kit. Links below go directly to Kit's own site. See our Disclosure page for details.

What makes it different

Kit's editor and automation tools are built around the idea of a creator selling something directly to their list — a course, a paid newsletter, a digital product — rather than a business running promotional campaigns. Tagging and segmentation are simpler and more visual than in most general-purpose email platforms, which makes subscriber journeys easier to actually understand at a glance.

Pricing

Kit offers a free plan for a limited subscriber count, with paid tiers scaling by list size. It sits in a similar price range to GetResponse and other creator-focused platforms once you're paying, though its free tier is more usable for a genuinely small list than most competitors.

Who it's built for

If your business model is "sell something directly to your own audience" — a newsletter, a course, coaching, digital products — Kit's tools are shaped around exactly that workflow. It's less suited to businesses running a traditional sales pipeline or managing client deals, where a CRM-style tool fits better.

Where it falls short

Kit's landing page and form builder are more limited than dedicated funnel tools like GetResponse — if building standalone landing pages is a priority, Kit is not the strongest option for that specific job on its own.

Who should skip it

If you're not selling directly to an audience — for example, if your business is service-based and email is mainly for client communication and lead nurturing rather than direct-to-audience sales — a general marketing platform will likely serve you better.

Verdict

Worth it for newsletter writers, course creators, and independent creators building a direct relationship with their audience. Skip it if your email needs are closer to lead nurturing and client communication than direct audience monetization.
